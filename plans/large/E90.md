## 2. Stakeholder brief and decision

Framed as a **retrospective review**, not a replay of a real commission. The fictional reader is the
pandemic planning unit of a German state health ministry, asked after the fact:

> In winter 2021-22 our measures hung on the 7-day hospitalisation incidence crossing 3, 6 and 9 per
> 100,000, read from the first published number. That number later roughly doubled. For next time:
> which signal should drive an alert, and at what probability, given what we would accept in false
> alarms and late calls?

**Decision.** One recommended alert rule per state: a signal, an alert probability, and a release
rule, stated as a false-alarm and late-call trade-off. Candidates: frozen value, revised value of a
week ago, frozen times a factor learned so far, chain-ladder ratio, PyMC nowcast probability.

**The objection the notebook must state early.** The rules applied to the frozen value, so frozen
crossing 6 was never wrong in law. Asking whether *final* incidence crossed 6 is our reading of the
threshold as a statement about burden. A frozen 6 meant a final value near 11, and a state-specific
one: the median final/frozen ratio ran from 1.37 (Bremen) to 2.52 (Hamburg) (measured). That is the
real finding about the frozen rule: its bias could be stated in advance, and it differed by state.

**Scope with E38.** Open by saying what E38 covers (triangle, vintages, hazard nowcast, renewal
equation, under-covering intervals, fog animation, icon array) and do not teach it again. No renewal
step here: this drops the triangular solve and the assumed delay distributions.

### Definitions

- **Frozen** on day *a*: 7-day sum over dates *a*-6..*a*, each as known on *a*.

- **Evaluation window:** as-of 2021-09-15 to 2022-04-29, 227 days, 3,632 state-days. Thresholds were

- **Crossing:** final at or above tau for 3 days after 3 days below.

## 6. The model

Hierarchical nowcast of the 16 states jointly, in PyMC. About 700 parameters (estimate).

$$n_{s,t,b} \sim \operatorname{NegBinomial}(\lambda_{s,t}\, p_{s,t,b},\ \phi_s)$$

- **Cells:** counts from `asof_nonnegative`, so nothing after the as-of date moves a cell; only cells
  fully known on the as-of date enter. Likelihood written centred.
- **Delay bins:** days 0-14 singly, then 15-21, 22-28, 29-42: 18 bins. Late cells are noisy and cost
  gradients without adding much.
- **Tail to 80 days:** a 70-day window cannot see delay 43-80, so multiply by a tail ratio per state
  from rows already complete on the as-of date, with Gamma uncertainty, plugged in. The window then
  stays fixed in shape, and the 1-6% tail is not ignored.
- **Level:** $\log\lambda_{s,t}$ integrates a weekly growth path: a national random walk plus state
  deviations, partially pooled, plus a Meldedatum weekday effect, `ZeroSumNormal` by state.
- **Hazard:** $\operatorname{logit} h_{s,t,b} = \gamma_b + \gamma^{s}_{b} + \rho_{s,\mathrm{wd}(t+b)} +
  \beta_{\mathrm{week}(t)} + \beta^{s}_{\mathrm{week}(t)}$: delay baseline by random walk, low-rank
  state offsets (3 basis functions, so about 3 per state), a reporting-weekday effect for daily bins,
  and weekly national and state drift.
- **Priors:** E38's hazard scales as a start, then prior predictive on the 7-day total. `LogNormal`, not
  half-normal, for any scale that shows E86's tail trouble. Sampler starting point: nutpie default,
  then `nuts={"adaptation": "low_rank"}` as in E38.

**Output:** per state and as-of date, draws of final 7-day incidence, so $P(\text{final} \ge \tau)$
for tau 3, 6, 9.

**Daily update between fits.** For each draw from the last fit, keep the delay parameters and tail
ratio, and take the new day's reports as data. With a Gamma working prior for each new row's total,
the remaining count is negative binomial in closed form. NumPy, seconds. This is how a ministry would
run it: refit weekly, update daily. It uses nothing from after the as-of day. Gated by P6.

**Variants:**
1. State, all ages: the main model.
2. Hindsight: the same, but delay parameters fixed from a fit on the finished triangle.
3. State x age, 96 strata, on 8 hard dates, summing to all-age. Age effects share the state's weekly
   path: about 1,300 parameters against the survey's 3,000 (estimate). Age is a pooling aid only,
   because the thresholds act on all-age values.

No per-date LOO: cells are nested by row and PSIS is not the question. Score held-out cells instead.

**Calibration:** score CRPS and 50%, 80%, 95% coverage of final 7-day incidence, per state. Any
recalibration may use only as-of dates whose truth was known by then: at least 80 days old, which
means nothing is available before December 2021. Say so.

**Predictive check:** E86's error must not recur. A check that reuses the fitted $\lambda$ on known
cells cannot fail, so the check here predicts cells revealed after the as-of date, without a refit.

## 8. Failure, fix, and what happens if it does not appear

**Failure 1: the convenient file knows the future (certain, measured).** Cut the preprocessed triangle
at the diagonal on 2022-01-20: Bremen shows 94 where 136 was published.
*Fix:* the unprocessed triangle and as-of cleaning. Be fair to E38, which said its cut was "very
close to, not identical": true outside Bremen. The notebook shows Bremen's frozen value at 30.0 on
2022-01-11 against a final 19.0, so frozen is not always an underestimate. The data do not say why;
do not explain it beyond the negative cells.

**Failure 2: delays fitted in hindsight flatter the score (expected, not seen).** Variant 2 against
variant 1 on 8 dates. *If it does not appear* (CRPS within 5%): report that delays were stable enough
for hindsight to cost little in this window, and that the leak is confined to shocks. Lesson moves to
failure 1.

**Failure 3: intervals too narrow (expected from Wolffram et al., and E38 saw it; do not reteach).**
Coverage per state, against RKI's real-time 95% interval from December. *Fix:* state drift and
overdispersion first; recalibrate on earlier dates only if needed. *If coverage is fine:* say so and
credit the drift term by removing it on two dates.

**Failure 4: the better forecast is the worse alarm (the real lesson, outcome open).** Choosing the
best CRPS and alerting at P = 0.5 may flap around a threshold: Schleswig-Holstein's final series
crossed 9 nine times in the hub window (measured). *Fix:* a chosen alert probability with a release
rule. *If the PyMC nowcast does not beat the factor on net benefit:* recommend the factor, say what
the model added (a probability, a stated false-alarm rate) and what that costs. This outcome is a
result and goes in the summary unchanged. Also report whether 96 strata changed any alert. If none,
that closes the question, and the state model is the recommendation.

**Sampler failure watch:** one bad date among 38 hiding in an average. Gate every fit on r_hat and ESS
of decision quantities, keep failed runs, restart once, list what was refitted.

## 10. Communication lesson

**A forecast is worth only what it changes at the threshold where someone acts.** The decision curve
turns "how accurate" into "at what false-alarm rate will you act, and what does that buy". Uncertainty
becomes something a ministry chooses: it picks an alert probability, not an interval. The notebook
states a known bias in advance, prints every call as said then and as seen later, and teaches one
reading skill: the x-axis is your cost ratio, $p_t/(1-p_t)$ false alarms you would accept per late
call avoided. Use "In plain words" boxes as in E38. Reader task at SMALL: change the Hamburg factor
window and watch the alert move, then explain the change in one sentence to a minister.
