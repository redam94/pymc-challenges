# E93 build plan: Where has the 1-in-20-year heat day become common?

Heat extremes on the whole sphere from ERA5: spherical harmonics, a smooth GEV, and a funded list
ranked by population exposure. PyMC-first: one PyMC model, nutpie on the small rung, its jaxified
logp under ChEES on the full rung.

**Status: CONDITIONAL.** The data route is better than the survey expected and is confirmed. The
sampler is unproven on dense data, and the big model must still show it changes the list (gate D1).

Number labels: **[M]** measured here on real samples; **[S]** from the survey, not re-measured;
**[X]** extrapolated by arithmetic; **[A]** assumption to be tested by a pilot.
No model has been fitted. Every runtime below is extrapolated.

## Stakeholder brief and decision

A public-health funder (fictional) can pay for heat-action plans in **40 urban regions**. The
director writes:

> We can fund 40. Tell me which 40, where the extra exposure to what used to be a rare hot day is
> largest. I want a reserve list, and I want to know which places are a coin-flip. A global model
> costs us real money: tell me whether it changed the list.

**Decision:** a funded list of 40, a named reserve, and the regions whose order cannot be separated.

**Primary metric**, for region R with cell population `pop(s)`:

    extra exposure(R) = sum over s in R of pop(s) * (p_now(s) - 0.05)    [person-events per year]

`T20(s)` is the level with a 5% annual chance in the 1980s climate. `p_now(s)` is the annual chance
of exceeding that same level in the 2021-25 climate. GISTEMP moved 0.831 K between those periods
(0.247 to 1.078, J-D means) [M, from `data/gistemp_glb.csv`].

**Rules the skeptics imposed:**
- Multiplier-only ranking favours low-variance climates by construction. The notebook shows that
  list beside the exposure list and reports how many regions differ.
- Rank regions, not neighbouring cities: a 1 degree cell is about 110 km.
- Region rule: each Natural Earth place with `pop_max` >= 1M goes to its nearest cell with land >= 0.5,
  places in touching cells merge, and the region is named after its largest place. Coastal cities
  therefore use the nearest land cell, and this is stated in the brief.
- Dry-bulb temperature is a weak proxy for health risk. The brief says so, and says the list is a
  screening list. Vulnerability and existing plans are outside the data.
- A cell maximum is not a thermometer reading, so only changes are compared with stations.
  Measured example: Sea-Tac's TMAX on 28 June 2021 is 42.2 C; its 1 degree cell reads 38.5 C at the
  first sampled hour, 16:00 solar time that day (the sample starts there, so the cell's own peak
  may be higher) [M].

## The model

Written in PyMC. `pm.Data` is frozen with `freeze_dims_and_data` before sampling.

    y(s,t) ~ SmoothGEV(q(s,t), sigma(s), xi(s))
    q(s,t) = m(s) + a(s) + b(s) (G(t) - G_mean) + u_t(s)     q is the median
    log sigma(s) = l0 + c * land_fraction(s) + f_sigma(s)
    xi(s) = -0.005 - 0.445 * expit(x0 + f_xi(s))             in (-0.45, -0.005)
    u_t(s) = year field, independent between years

- **`m(s)`**: each cell's median annual maximum, subtracted first. This removes orography, which
  L=60 cannot resolve, and centres the likelihood. The decision depends on `b`, `sigma` and `xi`,
  not on the level, so the small cost in uncertainty for `a` is acceptable and stated.
- **`G(t)`**: smoothed GISTEMP, so El Nino years go to `u_t`, not to `b`.
- **Median parameterisation**: location, scale and shape correlate within a cell, and modelling the
  median reduces that. `mu = q - sigma * ((log 2)^(-xi) - 1) / xi`.
- **Fields**: real spherical harmonics, Matern spectrum `(kappa^2 + l(l+1))^-(nu+1)`, nu = 3/2,
  normalised to unit variance. L=60 for `a`, `b`, `sigma`; L=15 for `xi`; L_year from P1.
- **Land modes**: fix the spectrum, take the SVD of `Phi_land S^(1/2)`, keep the leading modes.
  These are Slepian-like. The rotation keeps z ~ N(0, I), and modes dropped are exactly prior.
  Consequence: a free lengthscale needs a new SVD, so the FULL rung fixes it from the data's
  spectrum, and SMALL samples it to show the fixed value is reasonable.
- **Scale priors are LogNormal**: half-normal let a log sd run to -4e7 under MEADS in E86.
- **No boundary**: unlike E86's HSGP, the prior variance is the same everywhere: pointwise variance
  was constant to 6 digits at L=20 [M], and the sphere Gram error was 1e-13 [M]. The E86 c=1.25
  failure cannot occur. Land edges are not boundaries, only places with no data.

**Smooth GEV.** Upper-blended: GEV below `q(0.99)`, Gumbel above `q(0.999)`, Beta(5,5) weight
between, Gumbel fitted to those two quantiles. Below `q(0.99)` it is exactly the GEV, so the 1-in-20
level is untouched. The GEV is only evaluated at `min(y, q(0.999))`, always inside its support, so
`pt.switch` NaN gradients cannot arise, and xi stays away from 0, so no Taylor branch is needed.

Checked in NumPy for xi from -0.45 to -0.005 [M]: mass 1.000000; density positive across the blend;
`F(q95)` = 0.950000; log density finite 1 unit past the old endpoint; float32 term error at most
1.8e-5. Not yet checked: gradients in PyTensor and JAX (P2).

**Float32 sum.** 717,737 of these terms [M]: true -1,050,198.7; pairwise -1,050,198.8; running sum
**-1,050,777**, off by 578 nats. Whichever order XLA uses must be tested (P2). Fix: sum per cell, subtract a
per-cell constant from a reference point, then sum, through `pm.Potential` on the GPU path.

**Decision draws.** `u_t` must be integrated out and never reused. In each draw `T20(s)` and
`p_now(s)` use Gauss-Hermite nodes over the year field's marginal N(0, tau^2(s)), because the
marginal is not GEV. Predictive checks draw new year fields.

**Ladder**, each step priced and each asked whether the list moved:
1. Pooled shape and scale.
2. Local fits: 60 regions, shared xi, no spatial model. Under a minute [A]. The verdict's comparator.
3. Independent cells: L=60, no year field.
4. Year field added.
5. Land modes.

## Failure and fix

**Failure 1, headline: the wall.** A plain GEV with xi < 0 has a hard upper endpoint. With about
718,000 terms and a record year near it, a trajectory crossing any one endpoint gets infinite logp,
and in E86 one stuck chain could inflate the settings of all chains.
- Fix: the smooth GEV. Show the same model with both likelihoods, same starts.
- Candidate exhibit: the Pacific Northwest, June 2021.
- Store the failed run.
- *If it does not appear*: P1's endpoint check says how often data exceed a fitted endpoint.
  Under 0.1% of cells means the wall is rare at this scale. Then report that honestly, keep the
  smooth GEV as cheap insurance at its measured cost, and move Failure 2 to the lead.

**Failure 2, headline: confident and wrong.** Independent cells count one heatwave thousands of times.
- Test: the distribution of "number of cells over T20 in a year", from newly drawn year fields,
  against ERA5; and held-out GHCN stations' exceedance of their own 1980s level.
- Fix: the year field.
- *If residual correlation survives the year field* (P1): raise L_year, or temper the likelihood
  by an effective-sample ratio, and say the intervals are calibrated rather than exact.
- *If the independent model is already calibrated on the list*: rank intervals are the same; say so.
  That would argue against the big model and feeds D1.

**Failure 3: half the basis is blind.** At 1.5 degrees, L=20 and L=40, 47% and 56% of the land-Gram
eigenvalues are below 0.01, and only about 26% are above 0.5 [M]. At L=60, 585 land modes hold 99%
of the variance of a 1000 km field and 1,072 hold 99.9%; for 500 km, 984 and 1,270; all of 3,721
[M, 1.5 degree mask]. Expect a diagonal metric to struggle on the raw basis.
- Fix: land modes.
- *If raw mixes as well*: land modes still cut the arithmetic, but it is a speed gain, not a rescue.

**Failure 4, small: the stripe.** Over 3 days at 20-60N, the 6-hourly daily maximum falls short by
0.39-1.3 K by 30 degree band, mean 0.64 K. The lowest bands are 30-60E, 120-150E, 210-240E and 300-330E, as a 14:30
solar peak predicts (37E, 127E, 217E, 307E); the fit has a 0.33 K cos(4 lon) amplitude, R2 0.19 [M, three days only].
- This shifts level. `T20` and `p_now` use the same sampling, so most of it should cancel.
- Show the map from both samplings, then count regions that change.
- *Expected outcome*: a visible artefact that changes few regions, which is the lesson: harmless
  for a relative threshold, harmful for "days over 35 C". If it does move the list, it becomes a
  headline failure, and the hourly build pays for itself.

## Communication lesson

Say the change in people and odds, not degrees: "about N million more person-events a year above a
level that was once a 1-in-20", with its interval and as-of date. A multiplier alone favours places
with little variance, and a degree alone hides that 1 K is a lot in some climates. Name the coin-flips.
A list with a stated reserve is more honest than 40 confident ranks.

The notebook also states what the big model changed. If it changed little, that is the finding.
