## 1. Verdict and why it is conditional

The hardware case and the data are sound. The doubt is whether the network changes the answer.

- The density inside the authors' bouton mask is 0.086 blobs per um^2 per frame [measured, 41 frames
  of recording 7, simple blob detector]. E58's *sparse* simulation has 0.089 per um^2.
- In that simulation E58's recall was 0.927 for thresholding and 0.970 for its Bayesian pipeline, and
  in the dense one (0.70 per um^2) it was 0.531 and 0.758 [measured in E58's executed notebook].
- So at this density the classical pipeline may leave little for a network to gain. Early frames are
  denser (about 2.9x the end of the movie) and merged blobs hide some overlaps, so this is an
  indication, not a result. Under a Poisson assumption about 7% of blobs in the mask have a neighbour
  within 4 px [extrapolated]; the detector sees 0.7% because it merges them [measured].

Therefore the classical pipeline runs on the full movie first (pilot P2). The condition for building
is an owner decision, Q1: accept a notebook whose headline may be "the network did not change the
frames decision, and here is the certificate that shows why".

Second, the stake is smaller than the survey implied. A full recording takes **176 s** of camera time
[measured from the TIFF tags: 15,000 x 11.742 ms]. The brief is written around that.

## 2. Stakeholder brief and decision

From the lead of an imaging core, to the analyst:

> We are planning a repeat of the Unc-13 potentiation experiment. Last time each field was 15,000
> frames. On the camera that is only three minutes, but with 45 junctions per channel [paper: 22 + 23
> NMJs; one recording each is my assumption] it is 2.2 hours of scope time per channel and 54 GB per
> channel, and the dye bleaches throughout. For these two recordings, how many frames would have given
> the same answer? How far apart are the two? And I keep being told to replace our fitter with a neural
> network: would that change your answer, and what would I have to check before trusting one?

**Decision:** keep 15,000 frames, or cut to N for this preparation, and adopt the network or not.

**"Same answer", fixed before any full result is seen (owner to confirm, Q2).** N is the smallest
frame count at which, in *both* recordings, inside the mask:

1. FRC resolution is within 10% of its 15,000-frame value, and
2. the cluster count is within 10% of its 15,000-frame value, and
3. the block-bootstrap band at N overlaps the band at 15,000.

**What the answer may not claim (skeptics).** Two fields of one preparation with one fluorophore pair
cannot set a lab policy, and between-sample variance is not estimable. The second recording is the only
replication. Truncating a movie keeps the labelling and bleaching history of a full acquisition, so it
is a proxy for a shorter one. There is no ground truth and there are no dark frames. All advice is
conditional on this preparation, and the notebook says so next to the number.

The survey's "posterior band" on resolution is relabelled. It is a time-block bootstrap with
localisation error propagated, which is what it really is. It is not a posterior over resolution.

## 5. The model

**Classical baseline.** E58's pipeline (detection, batched Laplace fits, chosen K) on the full movie,
with its `detect` using a rolling temporal background rather than a fixed one, because the background
falls by a third. A CPU job pool, `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` set before NumPy. Same rule as the paper's
12,000 A/D filter reported alongside, so the baseline is the well-configured one.

**Network.** A DECODE-style design written fresh from the paper. A U-Net takes 3 consecutive frames
(offset-subtracted, scaled to counts) and outputs at each pixel: detection probability p; sub-pixel
x, y with log sds; log photons with log sd; background. Base 32, about 470,000 weights, depth to be
confirmed in P5. Receptive field about 44 px, which covers PSF wings and local background on 64 px crops.

Loss: a detection count term plus the Gaussian-mixture term over the true emitter set, as in the paper.
Optax Adam, gradient clipping, 64 x 64 crops, batch 16. Float32 throughout; no sum over large energies
is involved. Set `jax_enable_x64` to False explicitly. The training script does not import PyMC or
blackjax (either would change x64 or start CUDA).

**What it is not.** Per-pixel Gaussians are marginals. They are not a joint posterior over emitter
sets, and two nearby emitters' errors are not modelled. The sum of p is a count estimate whose
calibration must be shown. The network's posterior is conditional on the simulator's prior over density
and brightness, which is the whole point of the certificate.

**Simulator (GPU, inside the JIT).** Emitters from E58's forward model, erf-pixelated, then EMCCD noise.
JAX's Poisson and gamma samplers use rejection loops, which is the hardware skeptic's objection 2. The
measurements below answer it for typical pixels: median background is above 88 photons, so a fixed-cost
cube-root (Wilson-Hilferty) transform of a normal draw is close. It is weaker on the darkest pixels
(row 20), which P4 must check against exact draws:

| Photons in pixel | TV floor | Gaussian | Wilson-Hilferty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.004 | 0.059 | 0.021 |
| 88 | 0.004 | 0.028 | 0.009 |
| 136 | 0.005 | 0.023 | 0.009 |
| 2,000 | 0.004 | 0.008 | 0.005 |

[Measured against exact Poisson-Gamma plus read noise, 2,000,000 NumPy draws, gain 6.93, read sd 5.
Read sd is E58's assumption.]

Priors, version A (Gaussian): flat background, Gaussian PSF sd about 1 px with sd 20%, E58's brightness,
independent frames. Version B (empirical): section 7.

**Real data used for training (version B only):** PSF and background from recording 7 only. Recording
32 is never used for PSF, background, hyperparameters or tuning. It is opened once per network.

**PyMC role.** E58's NUTS spot model on 200 spots from the mask, split between isolated and paired, on
the CPU. The comparison is sd ratio and position agreement with the network, on real pixels. A separate
process, `JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu`, nutpie, BLAS set to one thread. nutpie stores all free variables, which is
trivial at this size.

E86 lessons that do not apply: MEADS, ChEES, HSGP boundaries and float32 energy sums have no role here.
Those that do: XLA dead-code removal, x64 set explicitly, detection through `bigrun.gpu_present`, BLAS
threads, no reuse of fitted latents in predictive checks, an honest CPU baseline, and no prose before
output is seen.

## 6a. Pilot results

### P1, 2026-11-01: linked classical fits on the cached crop

Run: `tools/build_e88_data.py p1`, seed 88, cores 3,4 (2 threads), no GPU, no download. Fits 37 s, whole
pilot 79 s with the simulation and bootstraps, peak 0.49 GB. The plan's 30 s covers about the fits only.
Numbers: `results/E88/p1_links.json`. Everything is [measured] on this crop unless labelled. The crop is
frames 3000-4999, lies outside the authors' mask, and is one region of one recording.

Check that the copied code matches E58: 4,192 blobs and K counts [15, 2022, 930, 440, 785], the same as
E58's printed output.

**Method, fixed in the code before any result was seen.** E58's detector and fits, with its 30 nm filter,
and localisations within 2 px of the crop edge dropped. Links are mutual nearest neighbours between
frames, radius 1.5 px (190 nm). The z sd is estimated by a mixture on the disc: a truncated 2-D normal of
variance c^2 s^2 for true links, uniform for false ones. c is the z sd. The 95% interval is a bootstrap
over tracks, because pairs in one chain share a fit. "Chosen K" is E58's pipeline, "K = 1" fits one
emitter to every blob. The tables below are for gap 1 unless they say otherwise.

| Quantity, radius 1.5 px | Chosen K | K = 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Localisations after the filter | 4,065 | 3,396 |
| Linked pairs, gap 1 / 2 / 3 | 1,685 / 920 / 684 | 1,452 / 770 / 540 |
| Tracks at gap 1 | 943 | 816 |
| True pairs from the mixture, gap 1 / gap 3 [estimated] | 1,580 / 563 | 1,255 / 405 |
| z sd, gap 1 (95% interval) | **1.39** (1.33-1.45) | **1.24** (1.17-1.31) |
| z sd, gap 3 (95% interval) | 1.37 (1.28-1.49) | 1.26 (1.16-1.36) |
| 95% half-width, gap 1 / gap 3 | 0.061 / 0.109 | 0.069 / 0.098 |
| Bootstrap se, gap 1 | 0.031 | 0.036 |
| 1.4826 MAD of z, no mixture, gap 1 | 1.40 | 1.35 |

**Against the gate.**

- **Pair count passes:** 1,685 against a required 500, and well clear of the 300 no-go line.
- **Precision is marginal.** The code read "+-0.05" as the 95% half-width, and that gives 0.061, so it
  misses narrowly. Read as one se (0.031) it passes. The plan did not say which, and that should not
  be settled after seeing the number (Q7). Passing the 95% reading needs about 2,500 pairs [extrapolated, 1 / sqrt n].
- **The no-go did not fire.** The paired test stays. The plan had no action for this middle case.
- **Full recordings should be well inside +-0.05:** the mask holds about 25x the crop's blobs in
  recording 7 and 16x in recording 32 [extrapolated from the 552,000, 356,000 and 19% above]. Half-widths
  at gap 3 would be about 0.02-0.03 [extrapolated, assuming the crop's link rate holds in the mask].
- **The crop cannot decide at gap 3.** Half-width 0.11 is wider than the 0.10 between the pass and fail
  lines. So the SMALL rung can teach the paired test but cannot give its verdict.

**Is the estimator sound? A simulated control.** One movie, with blinks at resampled real sites and
brightnesses, the crop's median background, stay-on probability 0.55, and E58's forward model. The truth
is known, and the model is right by construction, so this tests the method and says nothing about the
microscope.

| Same pipeline, gap 1, radius 1.5 px | Chosen K | K = 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Linked pairs | 2,083 | 1,884 |
| z sd from the mixture (95% interval) | 1.11 (1.08-1.13) | 1.06 (1.02-1.09) |
| z rms / 1.4826 MAD on the truly linked pairs | 1.14 / 1.08 | 1.44 / 1.06 |
| True-link share: mixture / truth | 0.984 / 0.978 | 0.941 / 0.951 |

The mixture recovers the known answer to within what the two direct measures disagree by. K = 1's rms
of 1.44 comes from outliers, and is 1.19 without |z| over 8.

**What P1 found beyond the gate: the classical error bars fail on real data.**

| z sd, gap 1 | Real | Simulated |
|---|---|---|
| Chosen K | 1.39 (1.33-1.45) | 1.11 (1.08-1.13) |
| Pairs where both blobs chose K = 1 | 1.12 (1.05-1.19), 369 pairs | 1.04 (1.01-1.07), 1,336 pairs |
| Pairs with any K of 2 or more | 1.47 (1.41-1.55), 1,316 pairs | 1.22 (1.18-1.29), 747 pairs |
| K = 1 on every blob | 1.24 (1.17-1.31) | 1.06 (1.02-1.09) |

- **Real and simulated separate clearly,** so the test has power: 0.28 for chosen K and 0.18 for K = 1.
- **Chosen K is over the 1.25 failure line** written for the network. K = 1 sits in the undefined zone
  between 1.15 and 1.25, and its interval crosses 1.25. Those lines were not written for classical fits.
- **The real data also pick K of 2 or more far more often:** 51% of blobs against 17% in simulation.
  Most real pairs are therefore in the worse row. Why those fits understate error is not established.
- **The answer moves with the radius on real data only.** Radii 0.75 / 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 3 px give 1.33 /
  1.35 / 1.39 / 1.41 / 1.43 for chosen K and 1.16 / 1.19 / 1.24 / 1.27 / 1.31 for K = 1, while simulation
  stays at 1.10-1.11 and 1.05-1.07. So the real displacements have heavier tails than the mixture
  allows, and this is not an artefact of the estimator. For K = 1 the range, 0.15, is as wide as the
  pass band, so the systematic matters more than the statistical error.
- **Part of the excess looks like a fixed floor rather than a scale.** Fitting c with an extra per-axis
  sd in the frame difference gives, on real data, c = 1.11 (1.06-1.19) and 4.2 nm (2.9-5.7) for K = 1,
  and c = 1.33 (1.27-1.40) and 3.4 nm (1.0-4.7) for chosen K. Simulated K = 1 gives c = 1.05 and 1.1 nm
  (0-2.1), and simulated chosen K gives no floor. That is about 2.9 nm per frame for K = 1 [extrapolated,
  divided by sqrt 2]. The cause is not identified. Consistent with it, the K = 1 third with the
  smallest error bars (s under 6.6 nm) has z sd 1.39, against 1.17 for the largest third. So z depends
  on the brightness mix.

False links: the mixture gives 6.2% at chosen K and 13.6% at K = 1, and gap 3 is worse, at 18% and 25%.
Links at a 200-frame gap, an upper bound because a molecule can return, number 156 against 1,685 (9.3%).

**What P1 does not show.** It says nothing about the network, the mask, other frames or recording 32. A
pass would still say nothing about a bias common to both frames.

### Verdict after P1

**Overall: still conditional go.** The condition is Q1, which P1 does not touch. Three things change:

1. The paired test stays, and it is now shown to separate real from simulated data.
2. Outcome C ("no failure") is less likely for the classical side, which already fails. Whether the

3. The notebook needs the classical z as a reference. A network at 1.3 would be no worse than the

## 7. Failure and fix

**Expected failure: sim-to-real.** Network A trained on E58's forward model. On simulation it should
reach near-nominal coverage. On real frames it inherits misfit that the simulation cannot show. This is
a hypothesis: E58's 51% is a Laplace evidence result outside the mask, and not a network measurement.

Checks on real frames. None may reuse the fitted emitters of the frames being judged:

1. **Paired frames (no ground truth needed).** A molecule on in frames t and t+3 gives two estimates of
   the same point. Their difference should be N(0, sd1^2 + sd2^2). The gap is 3 because the network
   sees three frames, so t+1 would not be independent; E58's fits are independent per frame and can use
   t+1. Pass: z sd within 1.0-1.15. Failure: over 1.25. This tests the *variance*. A bias common to both
   frames cancels, so a pass is necessary, not sufficient, and the notebook must say so. Linking needs
   a radius well above the sds and a mixture for false links. From P1: use its estimator (`link_pairs`,
   `fit_z_scale`, bootstrap over tracks), and one fixed radius for every method. Show the radius curve,
   the classical z and a simulated baseline beside the number. Compare on matched brightness, since z
   depends on it. At the time of writing the classical fits themselves are at 1.24-1.39 (section 6a),
   and the lines above are under review (Q8).
2. **Companion halo.** Density of faint detections around bright ones, against distance. E58's companions
   sat at 301-462 nm (quartiles). Failure: excess over 2x the far-field density in that ring.
3. **Population replicate.** Simulate new frames from the fitted *population* (density, brightness, PSF,
   background), not the frames' fitted emitters, and compare blob size, peak and radial profile.
   Include a lineup. Rendering fitted emitters back on to their own frame is reported only as an
   in-sample diagnostic, because it would look good by construction.
4. **NUTS audit** on 200 real spots: median sd ratio within 0.8-1.25. NUTS shares the forward model
   with the Laplace fit, so it can be expected to pass while the paired z fails [estimated]. It checks the
   approximation, not the model, so it does not replace check 1.

**Fix: network B.** Stack isolated bright spots from recording 7 for an empirical PSF with wings and a
range of defocus; sample real background maps from block medians, with flips; include persistence
across frames with on-probability randomised over 0.3-0.7 (the measured 0.46-0.61 sits inside);
randomise density over 0.02-0.8 per um^2. Warm-start from A with 30% of the steps. Judge on recording 32,
which played no part in the fit.

**Guaranteed second failure.** Random-split FRC on linked data. With about half of blobs persisting,
one blink lands in both halves, so resolution looks better than it is. The fix is to merge linked
localisations into events, then split by time blocks. Molecules that return after a long dark period
can still appear in both halves, so even this is optimistic. The notebook says so, and shows the gap
between the two curves. This failure is independent of the network, and does not depend on outcome A.

## 9. Communication lesson

A trained network is an instrument, and it ships with a **calibration certificate**: what it was trained
on, the range of photons, density and background tested, where coverage holds on simulation, where real
data agree, and where it was never tested. The notebook issues one on a single page. Three plain points:

- A simulation pass says the network learned the simulator. It says nothing about the microscope.
- Training cost and use cost are different bills. Separate them, and say who pays each.
- Report whether the expensive method changed the decision. If it did not, that is the finding.

**Reader tasks at SMALL level:** (1) change the simulator's density prior and watch the certificate
change; (2) rerun the paired-frame test at gap 1 and explain why the network now looks better than it
is; (3) replace the block split with a random split and measure the shift in FRC on the crop.
