### P3: rates

Measured on M1's NUMBA graph, which is the backend nutpie samples, at PyMC's start point. Limited to 1
and 2 BLAS threads under the 2-core rule, **so 3 threads was not tested and the plan's comparison is
incomplete by that much.**

| Setting | n = 419 | n = 1,030 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 thread, alone | 8.7 ms | 105.6 ms |
| 1 thread each, two copies side by side | 9.0 ms | 111.2 ms |
| 2 threads, alone | 5.3 ms | 60.6 ms |
| 2 threads each, two copies on 2 cores (oversubscribed) | 13.3 ms, p90 42 ms | 153 ms, p90 390 ms |

**Chains side by side with one thread each lose 4-5%, while 2 threads on one chain gain 1.6-1.75x, and
oversubscription is ruinous.** So fits use one BLAS thread a chain. The fit confirms this: 68.2 s over
7,340 gradients a chain is 9.3 ms in nutpie itself.

Raw rates replacing the plan's old base, 1 thread then 2: Cholesky, two solves and an inverse at n =
1,000: **50.6, 27.9 ms** (plan: 188). Cholesky at n = 2,000: 56.4, 33.5 ms, so 47 and 80 Gflop/s
(estimated, nominal flops); at n = 1,000 it is 40 Gflop/s on either. `matmul` at n = 1,000: 57 and 114
Gflop/s. `ndtr`: 11.4 ns (plan: 12.8). **A rate from a Cholesky does not cost a gradient:** the bench
rate implied 32-39 ms at n = 1,030 on 2 threads, and the real figure is 60.6 ms, because the kernel
and its derivative are not in that rate. The interim bench-based times in section 3 were replaced by
these. Conditionals took 93 ms a draw on SMALL with 1 BLAS thread, against about 27-32 ms estimated at the same 1-thread rate (the bench's 22-27 ms is a 2-thread rate).

### What changed in the design

- M2 is 80 x 50 at c = 1.5 (4,000 modes), not 40 x 25.
- VOI uses 200 draws, not 100, plus a simulated joint value for the stopping rule and figure 9.
- One BLAS thread a chain. 4 chains on 2 cores means two batches.
- M1 has 15 free parameters, not about 11.
- The `jax` assertion in section 9 is replaced. **As written it fails on every fit.** nutpie imports arviz,
  and `arviz_base.io_numpyro` calls `lazy_loader.load("jax")`, which leaves a stub in `sys.modules`. Real
  JAX does not run: tested through `import pymc`, `nutpie.sample`, `pm.sample`, `az.summary`, `az.rhat`,
  `online_core_check`, `banner` and `Store`, with one module present and no CUDA library mapped. **But any
  attribute access on the stub imports all of JAX (292 modules) and maps libcuda.** My own diagnostic
  did that by accident; the GPU was hidden and no device work ran. The check was replaced after seeing
  this failure, and it is labelled as such in `jax_executed`. `bigrun` now guards against the stub.
- Nothing was run on the stale rates. They were corrected in section 3 before P3, then replaced.

## 1. Stakeholder brief and decision

> *From a state air-monitoring planner, working with an EPA regional office.* We can fund a fixed
> number of new PM2.5 monitors. About four in five US counties have none, and they hold about 100
> million people. Which sites should we fund, how many are worth funding, and what would we be
> buying? We expect "put them where we are most uncertain" to be challenged, so we need to know
> whether that answer is right.

**The decision:** a ranked shortlist of sites, and the budget at which one more monitor stops
paying. Each site is ranked by how many people live in counties where it would change a wrong call
into a right one.

**Definition of a wrong call.** Attainment in law is decided from eligible monitors' 3-year design
values, so a county with no monitor has no call that could be wrong in law. The notebook says this
plainly. Its loss is on *exposure*: the latent 3-year mean PM2.5 at a county's population centre,
compared with 9.0 ug/m3 (and with 12.0). A wrong call means that the best current estimate is on the
other side of the line from the truth. Design values round to 0.1, so "above 9.0" means at least
9.05.

**Timing, stated up front.** EPA's designations rest on 2023-2025 data, so no new monitor can inform
this round. A monitor funded in 2027 and running by 2028 gives its first 3-year mean for 2028-2030
(extrapolated from the calendar). The decision is for the next review cycle.

- Warmup cannot be checkpointed inside a stage (about 85% of the gradients, per the survey). Two

## 4. The model

Everything in the annual path is PyMC with nutpie. JAX never executes, so CUDA never starts; arviz leaves a lazy stub in `sys.modules`, which `bigrun` now leaves alone.

Let `y_s` be the log window mean at site s, and `v_s` its known sampling variance (delta method on
the script's formula, finding 12).

```
y_s ~ Normal(alpha + beta_d log_density_s + w_s' beta_m + a_s' delta + f(s), tau[scale_s]^2 + v_s)
f   ~ GP(0, eta^2 Matern32(ell))     on Albers km
```

- `log_density_s`: county population density. It is the skeptic's minimum covariate, and it exists
  at candidate sites as well as monitors. `Location Setting` does not, so it stays out.
- `w_s`: share of days by method family, FRM as reference. Predictions are FRM-equivalent.
- `a_s`: which years the site has, a sum-to-zero offset `delta` (findings 6, 15).
- `tau[scale]`: one nugget per measurement scale. Microscale and source-oriented sites should not
  pretend to be neighbourhood means.
- **Marginalised:** `f` is integrated out, so `pm.MvNormal` on 1,030 sites with 15 free
  parameters (P4): no latent field to sample, and no nutpie storage problem. Exact conditionals follow per
  posterior draw. This replaces the survey's 45,000-parameter HSGP as the base model.

Priors: `ell` LogNormal around 400 km (sd 0.6 on the log scale); LogNormal or Gamma(2, .) on `eta`
and `tau`, not half-normals (E56, E86). All set from a prior predictive check in NumPy.

**Models.** M0: spatial field only, no density, no method term. M1: as written. M2: M1 plus a shared
field in a county-level Bernoulli "has a monitor" term, as a latent HSGP (Diggle et al. 2010, cited
from memory).

**HSGP rule (E86 S1).** Used in M2 and in the daily model, and the same check gates both: an edge
that loses variance is E86's failure again. County-centre half-widths are 2,262 x 1,419 km (measured).
Do not use the `approx_hsgp_hyperparams` route on a wide lengthscale range (AUTHORING); tabulate edge
variance and error against M1's exact field, and use `freeze_dims_and_data` if on `pm.Data`.

Prior variance of a unit field, east-west edge midpoint (corner in brackets), computed from the
basis with no fit. The `ell` values are the prior's 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 x 25 (M2) | 1.25 | 0.96 (0.95) | 0.95 (0.78) | **0.66 (0.33)** |
| 40 x 25 | 1.5 | 0.93 (0.93) | 0.99 (0.98) | 0.94 (0.75) |
| 40 x 25 | 2.0 | **0.86 (0.86)** | 0.98 (0.98) | 1.00 (0.97) |
| 22 x 14 (daily) | 1.25 | **0.81 (0.80)** | 0.93 (0.76) | **0.66 (0.33)** |

| 22 x 14 | 2.0 | **0.61 (0.63)** | 0.91 (0.92) | 0.99 (0.97) |

**P5 superseded the mode count: 40 x 25 fails the mean-field gate at fitted `ell`, and M2 is 80 x 50 at
c = 1.5.** The table stands for edge variance only. So **c = 1.25 fails at long `ell`**, as in E86. Start from c = 1.5. M2 then passes at every `ell`,
except at box corners, which are ocean or Canada, so the gate reads at edge midpoints and reports
corners. Raising c trades edge variance for truncation at short `ell`, so more c is not always
better. The daily 300 modes pass only if `ell` is well above 185 km. Recheck against fitted `ell`.

**Checks that must not reuse fitted fields (E86 S3).** Predictive checks draw a new field from the
GP at the fitted hyperparameters. Hold-out uses exact conditionals. `az.loo` sees one observation
from an `MvNormal`, so leave-one-out comes from the precision matrix (AUTHORING, E19-E23), plus
spatial block folds. Dense `MvNormal` under nutpie is not bit-reproducible: quote round numbers.

### Value of information (closed form, as the hardware skeptic asked)

For a posterior draw theta, the exact conditional gives county means `mu_t` and covariance `S`. A
new monitor at c with noise variance `n` has predictive variance `S_cc + n`. Hyperparameters stay
integrated over, because the post-monitor decision uses the mixture over draws:

1. For candidate c, take 16 nodes `y_j` on the mixture predictive of the reading (quadrature, not 64
   fantasies).
2. Reweight draws by their predictive density at `y_j`, update each draw's county mean by the
   rank-one formula, and form `p_t(y_j)`, the mean over draws of a normal probability.
3. Expected loss is the sum over j of `P(y_j)` times population-weighted `min(p_t, 1 - p_t)`.
4. Count only counties within 3 ell and with current p between 0.01 and 0.99. Greedy: pick the best,
   downdate every draw, repeat, rescoring lazily (section 3).
5. Use 200 draws (P6). The downdate keeps each draw's mean, since the reading is unknown, so only round
   one is an exact value. The value of a set comes from simulating its readings jointly (`check_batch`
   in `tools/pilot_e89.py`), never from summing gains. Sums ran up to 2.1x high (P6).

Candidates: the population centre of each unmonitored CONUS county, 2,452 (measured: 2,468 minus 16
registered). The monitor's noise comes from the fitted
`tau`, plus a continuous FEM's own small `v`. Dollars do not enter: the unit is **people moved from a
wrong call to a right one per monitor**, and the budget line is a planner's input (open question 3).

**Stated limits.** A county is one point, so a large county's internal gradients are ignored. There
are no satellite or low-cost-sensor covariates, so the field overstates the fog in empty counties.
The notebook names this (EPA's fused surfaces, satellite-derived products, sensor networks: products
named from memory) and treats the shortlist as a prior for those checks.

## 8. Communication lesson

**Value of information is one plain question: what would we do differently if we knew?** A map of
uncertainty and a map of decision value are different maps, and the difference is the lesson. The
notebook teaches the reader to say "this county is very uncertain and not worth a monitor" and
defend it with figure 8, and to state a stopping rule before the budget meeting. A second lesson
follows from gate P8: state whether the big model changed the decision. If it did not, that is the
result, and it goes in the opening price table.

Sentences the notebook must be able to say, if the outputs support them: which of the top 20 change
under each objective; what no new monitor can do for the February 2027 designations; and what a
planner with satellite data should check first.
