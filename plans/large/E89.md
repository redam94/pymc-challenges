# E89 build plan: Where should the next air monitors go?

Value of information for the US PM2.5 network. PyMC first: the annual CPU model comes first, and
the daily GPU model runs only if it moves the ranking.

**Status: conditional.** The annual notebook is a go once pilots P1-P4 pass. The daily GPU model is
doubtful: measurements below suggest that the annual model, given known measurement error, carries
most of what a daily model would add. If so, E89 is a CPU notebook, and that is its finding.

Status labels: **measured** (run on real files on 2026-11-01), **extrapolated** (arithmetic from a
measurement, never run), **assumed** (a stated guess), **unconfirmed** (from a search or memory).
No model has been fitted, so nothing here is a modelling result.

## 1. Stakeholder brief and decision

> *From a state air-monitoring planner, working with an EPA regional office.* We can fund a fixed
> number of new PM2.5 monitors. About four in five US counties have none, and they hold about 100
> million people. Which sites should we fund, how many are worth funding, and what would we be
> buying? We expect "put them where we are most uncertain" to be challenged, so we need to know
> whether that answer is right.

**The decision:** a ranked shortlist of sites, and the budget at which one more monitor stops
paying. Each site is ranked by how many people live in counties where it would change a wrong call
into a right one.

**Definition of a wrong call.** Attainment in law is decided from eligible monitors' 3-year design
values, so a county with no monitor has no call that could be wrong in law. The notebook says this
plainly. Its loss is on *exposure*: the latent 3-year mean PM2.5 at a county's population centre,
compared with 9.0 ug/m3 (and with 12.0). A wrong call means that the best current estimate is on the
other side of the line from the truth. Design values round to 0.1, so "above 9.0" means at least
9.05.

**Timing, stated up front.** EPA's designations rest on 2023-2025 data, so no new monitor can inform
this round. A monitor funded in 2027 and running by 2028 gives its first 3-year mean for 2028-2030
(extrapolated from the calendar). The decision is for the next review cycle.

**Legal status** (recheck every line at build, and keep the 12.0 results either way):

| Date | Event | Source status |
|---|---|---|
| 26 Jun 2026 | D.C. Circuit upholds the 9.0 standard | one secondary source; EPA confirms the outcome, not the date |
| 17 Jul 2026 | N.D. Cal. orders final designations by 6 Feb 2027 | EPA, law-firm summaries |
| 28 Aug 2026 | Two cert petitions filed by states and industry | EPA letters as summarised by search |
| 2 Sep 2026 | EPA appeals; Ninth Circuit grants expedited consideration | EPA bulletin of 2 Oct 2026, read |
| Oct 2026 | 120-day letters: 2023-2025 data, 30 days for state input, a separate 30-day public comment period | EPA bulletin, read |

No docket was read. EPA's bulletin says the appeals are pending, says it will try to meet the
deadline, and cites "concerns with the accuracy of air monitors", wildfires and new data. The
survey's 1 November comment date is unconfirmed: only "30 days" was seen.

**Objective is a choice.** A state may not want to find exceedances, so "fewest wrong calls" is not
automatically its objective. The notebook reports the shortlist under four objectives:
population-weighted wrong calls (headline), missed exceedances only, false exceedances only, and
unweighted wrong calls.

### What was confirmed, and what the survey did not know

All measured on the files above unless marked.

**Schema.** The daily file has 29 columns, including `Method Code` and `Event Type`. Event Type
values are the strings `None`, `Included`, `Excluded`. This settles the survey's "from memory" flag.
`Pollutant Standard` is `PM25 24-hour 2012` or blank (hourly). The annual file repeats each monitor
across 8 standards and 4 event types (`No Events`, `Events Included`, `Events Excluded`, `Concurred
Events Excluded`). Use `PM25 Annual 2024`.

**1. The file holds two versions of the same T640 instruments. This is the biggest data finding.**
EPA's advisory of 26 Aug 2024 (linked on the download page; absent from the survey) says to drop
methods 236 and 238. They make up 176,560 of 847,162 rows in 2023 (20.8%). At paired site-days:

| Pair | Pairs | Sites | Original | Corrected | Difference | Original above 9.0, corrected not |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 236 vs 736 | 55,784 | 260 | 10.62 | 9.46 | +1.16 | 47 sites |
| 238 vs 738 | 27,166 | 133 | 9.66 | 8.57 | +1.09 | 15 sites |

So **62 site-series change sides of 9.0 in 2023** depending on which version is used.

**2. The filter cannot be applied the way the advisory's annual-file note implies.** Original and
corrected data sit under different POCs (3 and 23 at the site inspected), and methods 636 and 638
start on 14 June 2023 (2023 file). The 2023 annual file carries no T640 method names at all: the
corrected POC's row has a blank method, and the others read `Multiple Methods Used` and match the data with the original series left in (median gap

**4. pandas reads `None` as NaN.** Without `keep_default_na=False`, a filter on Event Type kept

**5. Corrected counts for 2023:** 305,856 site-days, 1,012 sites, 634 counties, against the survey's

### FULL daily: only if P8 passes

A daily model exists to supply better site means and variances. It feeds **the same annual VOI
stage**, so the rank-200 ensemble covariance problem the skeptic raised never arises.

- Lattice: 925 sites x 1,096 days = 1.01M cells. Site-days: 2,346,920 rows x (305,856 / 847,162) =
  847,000, about 84% full. Use a mask on the full lattice, so no gather or scatter.
- 300 x 150 modes = 45,000 parameters, a design target. Per pass: 32 x (925 x 300 x 150 + 925 x 150
  x 1,096) = 6.2e9 mult-adds, double for reverse mode, 25-50 ms per gradient (scaled from E86).
- At least 60,000 gradients: 25-50 min. **This is a lower bound.** E86's gradient counts came from
  sparse data; here low-order modes are data-dominated and may be far stiffer.
- VRAM: 32 x 1.01M x 4 B = 130 MB per lattice array, about 10 live = 1.3 GB, plus the 7.5 GiB d x d
  accumulator unless `_length_floor=False` is used. Under 10 GB against 18 GB usable (75% of 24).
- Warmup cannot be checkpointed inside a stage (about 85% of the gradients, per the survey). Two
  stages give one natural restart point. On this machine that is the main risk.

## 4. The model

Everything in the annual path is PyMC with nutpie. No JAX is imported, so CUDA never starts.

Let `y_s` be the log window mean at site s, and `v_s` its known sampling variance (delta method on
the script's formula, finding 12).

```
y_s ~ Normal(alpha + beta_d log_density_s + w_s' beta_m + a_s' delta + f(s), tau[scale_s]^2 + v_s)
f   ~ GP(0, eta^2 Matern32(ell))     on Albers km
```

- `log_density_s`: county population density. It is the skeptic's minimum covariate, and it exists
  at candidate sites as well as monitors. `Location Setting` does not, so it stays out.
- `w_s`: share of days by method family, FRM as reference. Predictions are FRM-equivalent.
- `a_s`: which years the site has, a sum-to-zero offset `delta` (findings 6, 15).
- `tau[scale]`: one nugget per measurement scale. Microscale and source-oriented sites should not
  pretend to be neighbourhood means.
- **Marginalised:** `f` is integrated out, so `pm.MvNormal` on about 925 sites with about 11 free
  parameters: no latent field to sample, and no nutpie storage problem. Exact conditionals follow per
  posterior draw. This replaces the survey's 45,000-parameter HSGP as the base model.

Priors: `ell` LogNormal around 400 km (sd 0.6 on the log scale); LogNormal or Gamma(2, .) on `eta`
and `tau`, not half-normals (E56, E86). All set from a prior predictive check in NumPy.

**Models.** M0: spatial field only, no density, no method term. M1: as written. M2: M1 plus a shared
field in a county-level Bernoulli "has a monitor" term, as a latent HSGP (Diggle et al. 2010, cited
from memory).

**HSGP rule (E86 S1).** Used only in M2. Domain half-widths about 2,300 x 1,450 km. Do not use the
`approx_hsgp_hyperparams` route on a wide lengthscale range (AUTHORING); tabulate edge variance and
error against M1's exact field, and use `freeze_dims_and_data` if on `pm.Data`.

**Checks that must not reuse fitted fields (E86 S3).** Predictive checks draw a new field from the
GP at the fitted hyperparameters. Hold-out uses exact conditionals. `az.loo` sees one observation
from an `MvNormal`, so leave-one-out comes from the precision matrix (AUTHORING, E19-E23), plus
spatial block folds. Dense `MvNormal` under nutpie is not bit-reproducible: quote round numbers.

### Value of information (closed form, as the hardware skeptic asked)

For a posterior draw theta, the exact conditional gives county means `mu_t` and covariance `S`. A
new monitor at c with noise variance `n` has predictive variance `S_cc + n`. Hyperparameters stay
integrated over, because the post-monitor decision uses the mixture over draws:

1. For candidate c, take 16 nodes `y_j` on the mixture predictive of the reading (quadrature, not 64
   fantasies).
2. Reweight draws by their predictive density at `y_j`, update each draw's county mean by the
   rank-one formula, and form `p_t(y_j)`, the mean over draws of a normal probability.
3. Expected loss is the sum over j of `P(y_j)` times population-weighted `min(p_t, 1 - p_t)`.
4. Count only counties within 3 ell and with current p between 0.01 and 0.99. Greedy: pick the best,
   downdate every draw, repeat.

Candidates: the population centre of each unmonitored CONUS county, about 2,480 (assumed: 2,509
minus 17 registered, and changes with three-year data). The monitor's noise comes from the fitted
`tau`, plus a continuous FEM's own small `v`. Dollars do not enter: the unit is **people moved from a
wrong call to a right one per monitor**, and the budget line is a planner's input (open question 3).

**Stated limits.** A county is one point, so a large county's internal gradients are ignored. There
are no satellite or low-cost-sensor covariates, so the field overstates the fog in empty counties.
The notebook names this (EPA's fused surfaces, satellite-derived products, sensor networks: products
named from memory) and treats the shortlist as a prior for those checks.

### P8, the daily gate

Daily data can only change the ranking through inputs the annual stage already uses. So perturb
those inputs, which costs minutes and no GPU:

- **V1 oracle:** set `v_s = 0`. A perfect daily model could do no better on schedule error.
- **V2 events:** use concurred-excluded values where the annual file allows.
- **V3 hyperparameters:** fix `ell` at its 10th and 90th percentile.

**Run the daily model only if** in any variant the top-20 overlap with the baseline falls below 14,
or the stopping budget moves by more than 5 monitors. Otherwise the notebook prints the bound and
says the daily model was not run and why. **Limit of this gate:** V1 bounds schedule error only.
Daily data could also sharpen the field's covariance, and V3 only approximates that channel.

Expected thresholds are judgement calls (the owner may change them): 14 of 20 is about the level at
which a planner would notice a different list.

## 6. Failures the notebook expects to show

Each is kept only if it appears when measured.

**F1 (already seen): the naive table is wrong before any model runs.** Measured: 20.8% of 2023 rows
are the superseded series, reading 1.1 ug/m3 high, and 62 site-series switch sides of 9.0. The fix is
the POC stitch. The notebook also runs the dirty table through the SMALL model and reports how far
the ranking moves. **If the ranking barely moves:** the data lesson stands on its own, and the text
says that field smoothing hid an error that a site-level compliance reading would not.

**F2 (the main one): uncertainty is not value.** Greedy on posterior sd against greedy on EVSI. A
place can be highly uncertain and worthless to monitor, because no reading changes the call.
Expected: sd spends its monitors in empty clean counties. **If top-20 overlap is at least 14:** the
finding is that a population-weighted sd map is good enough here and the planner can skip EVSI;
report the price of each method and the map where the two disagree.

**F3: preferential siting.** Measured so far, a *crude comparison, not a test*: same-year difference
from the nearest older site, by distance.

| New site's distance | Sites | Mean | se | Median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| under 25 km | 31 | +0.09 | 0.28 | +0.09 |
| 25-50 km | 11 | -0.90 | 0.70 | -0.31 |
| over 50 km | 25 | -0.92 | 0.36 | -0.64 |
| over 25 km | 36 | -0.92 | 0.32 | -0.64 |

Far-away new sites read about 0.9 lower than their nearest neighbour, which is what an upward-biased
network would produce, but also what rural sites next to urban ones would produce. The backtest
holds out sites first seen in 2023 or later and compares M0, M1 and M2 on bias and coverage. With 36
sites the mean bias has a se of about 0.3, and 90% coverage is known to about 5 points (extrapolated).
**Only 4 of those 36 sit in the 8-10 band, so the backtest cannot validate wrong-call rates, and the
notebook must not claim it does.** **If M1's density term removes the bias:** preferential siting is
mostly population targeting, M2 is dropped, and the text says so. **If M2 does no better than M1:**
same. **If M2 fits but mixes badly:** report it as tried. The shared field is confounded with
population, so this is a real possibility.

**F4: plug-in hyperparameters.** Rerun VOI at the posterior median of theta. Keep this section only
if top-20 overlap falls below 16 or predictive sd is at least 15% too small (E82 measured about 24%).
**Otherwise** one line in the price table.

**Dropped from the survey.** The stuck-chain ChEES failure: there is no ChEES in the annual path. It
also ran through E86's length floor, so with the floor off it may not appear at all.

## 8. Communication lesson

**Value of information is one plain question: what would we do differently if we knew?** A map of
uncertainty and a map of decision value are different maps, and the difference is the lesson. The
notebook teaches the reader to say "this county is very uncertain and not worth a monitor" and
defend it with figure 8, and to state a stopping rule before the budget meeting. A second lesson
follows from gate P8: state whether the big model changed the decision. If it did not, that is the
result, and it goes in the opening price table.

Sentences the notebook must be able to say, if the outputs support them: which of the top 20 change
under each objective; what no new monitor can do for the February 2027 designations; and what a
planner with satellite data should check first.

## 12. Open questions for the owner

1. **Is a CPU notebook acceptable in this series?** If P8 stops the daily model, E89 needs no GPU
   and about 1.5-3.5 h of CPU (extrapolated). The honest title is then close to "the big model was
   not needed". Recommended, but it changes the series' mix.
2. **Is the gate acceptable?** It may conclude "no daily model" without one being run, and it
   bounds only some channels. The survey's skeptic asked to run both. If you want the comparison
   measured, the daily model needs a slot regardless, and P9 decides whether it is feasible.
3. **What does a monitor cost?** No figure has been sourced, and none will be invented. Without one,
   the stopping rule is people per monitor, with the planner's own line drawn on it.
4. **Wrong call with or without wildfire?** Default: events included, because smoke is exposure.
   Regulatory values exclude concurred events, and 39 of 190 monitors differ by over 0.5 in 2023.
5. **Extra covariates?** EPA's designations page lists county emissions and VMT files, of unchecked
   size. A satellite product would shrink the fog, but it needs its licence checked. My default is
   population density only, with the omission stated.
6. **Registered-only sites:** should the 17 counties with registered monitors and no data be
   excluded as candidates? Default excluded, until someone confirms what a blank date means.
7. **CONUS only?** Default yes. Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico are different regimes, and that needs
   saying to a national reader.
8. **Tone of the brief:** the designation process is contested and live. The plan keeps the brief
   neutral and factual. Tell me if you would rather move it to a fictional planner's framing only.
