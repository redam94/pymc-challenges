## 1. What the notebook teaches

Price = (time per gradient) x (gradients needed) + memory + precision + disk. The reader learns to
measure each term with the shared pilot in `bigrun.py`, on five cheap real models with different
cost shapes, then to read a price table and decline to buy.

## 2. Stakeholder brief and decision

The fictional research lead of a passenger-rights charity writes:

> US rules treat a flight that is more than 30 minutes late or cancelled over half the time, in a
> month, as chronically delayed, and holding one out for more than four consecutive months as a
> violation. We want a monthly watch list of the 50 flights most likely to be flagged next month. A
> contractor quotes a flight-level model on two years of every US flight: "two hours on one GPU,
> from a published rate". Our analyst says a table of counts will do. I have one workstation for
> one night a month, about 8 hours. **Do I approve the quote, buy something smaller, or refuse? Give me a price I can check
> and tell me whether the bigger model changes the list.**

The notebook ends with one of three answers, chosen by a rule fixed before any fit (section 5):
approve the row-level run, buy a named smaller rung, or stay with the counting rule.

The rule itself, read from the regulation: 14 CFR 399.81(c) defines a chronically delayed flight as
a domestic flight operated at least 10 times a month that arrives more than 30 minutes late,
cancellations included, more than 50 percent of the time in that month. Paragraph (c)(3) treats a
carrier's flights in a city-pair market within 30 minutes of the most frequent scheduled time as one
flight. Holding one out for more than four consecutive one-month periods is the violation. Diversions
are not mentioned.

The watch list is a statistical estimate, not a legal finding, and the notebook says so wherever
the list appears. It is not DOT's own computation.

floor, which blackjax's docstring credits with most of the diagonal metric's value. **R3 at full scale

## 5. The model

**The target.** For each rule flight operated at least 10 times in month t+1, the probability that
its late-or-cancelled rate exceeds 0.5. A flight's history comes from the trailing 12 months. Three
forecast origins: targets of 2025-04, 2025-07, 2025-10. The rest of 2024-25 is training.

| Rung | Likelihood and effects | Size | Engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | Persistence: flagged last month means flagged next month | Counts | NumPy |
| R1 | Beta-binomial on flight counts, conjugate, prior fitted per carrier | 15 priors | NumPy, seconds |
| R2 | Beta-binomial on flight x month cells; logit mean = carrier + origin + dest + month + flight | 246.5-247.1k eligible cells a window [M]; d 73-78k with every flight, about 32k with the cut below [X] | nutpie; ChEES on one origin, cut only |
| R3 | Bernoulli on rows; adds origin x day, dest x day, tail | 14,080,680 rows [M], d about 747k [X] | Priced only at full scale; nutpie on one carrier |

**R2 flight effects are cut for ChEES.** 73-78k parameters need 19.9-22.7 GiB for the ChEES diagonal
[X], over the 16.56 GiB limit, and 35-40% of the flights in a window have one eligible cell [M].
Flights with at least 3 eligible cells in the window get their own effect: 30.7-31.5k flights
holding 75-76% of the cells [M], so d about 32k and 3.8 GiB [X]. The rest take only the carrier,
origin and destination terms. nutpie can run the uncut model, and P9 must price both. A flight with
no history gets no flight effect, and 11-25% of the target flights have none (section 12).

R2 details: `pm.ZeroSumNormal` for carrier and month; LogNormal on sds, since E86 saw half-normal
priors on sds run away under MEADS; flight effects tried centred and non-centred at the pilot,
because flights with many operations are data-dominated. Beta-binomial `phi` carries overdispersion
between months. A future month needs a new month effect and overdispersion draw.

R3's reason to exist is measured: the national daily share ran from 0.094 to 0.58 in one month, and
a day's weather hits many flights at once. Whether it matters for a monthly list is the question.

**Choice of rung, first version.** The notebook's one-carrier rule replaced this one before any score
was read: a list of 10 sharing at least 8, with 50 printed only, and the overlap taken at one carrier
(section 13). Score held-out flag forecasts by mean log score and by precision among
the top 50. Buy the cheapest rung whose log score is within one bootstrap SE of the best, resampling
origin airports, and whose top 50 overlaps the next rung's by 80% or more. Report all three origins
separately: three origins cannot support a pooled interval and the notebook does not claim one. R3
competes only on its one carrier, against R2 restricted to the same flights, and that limit is stated.

**Checks that must not repeat E86's errors.**
- Predictive checks redraw month effects and overdispersion; a check that reuses fitted latents proves
  nothing (S3).
- Check month-to-month and carrier-level spread, since a check on totals cannot fail.
- `r_hat` on the decision quantities as well as on parameters.
- Compare with R0 and R1 always; they may win, and if so that is the finding.

## 7. Failure, fix, and what happens if the failure does not appear

**Expected failure.** The contractor's rate is wrong in kind. Reverse mode through `beta[idx]` turns a
gather into a scatter-add with non-unique indices. E86 had no indexed effects, so its 0.336 ns does not
transfer. The bench did not show a slow scatter in its easy case (one chain, random indices, default
flags): about 0.047 ns per element added [B, estimated]. That does not settle it. Whether the scatter is slow with
8 chains and real indices, and how the plugin's `xla_gpu_enable_scatter_determinism_expander`
changes that (flag name confirmed in the installed plugin; default and cost unknown), is the pilot.

**Variants timed.**

| Variant | What changes |
|---|---|
| `auto_unsorted` | Plain `beta[idx]`, rows in file order |
| `auto_sorted` | Rows sorted by flight, sorted hints passed where JAX accepts them |
| `bucket` | `custom_vjp`: the transpose is a pure gather over a table with rows in power-of-2 buckets |
| Collapse (R2) | No code trick: fewer rows through algebra. 13.84 rows per cell in one month [M] |

The bucket table is 685,557 entries for 547,271 rows of 2024-01 on the flight-number key, 1.25x [M]; plain
padding would need 8.8M entries for origin, 16x [M]. The other obvious fix, a sorted cumulative sum,
is excluded: in float32 a running sum over 14M terms loses the small group sums, and that is the
trap to name in one line.

**The second failure, certain as arithmetic.** At 14M rows |logp| is about 6.8e6 [X], float32 spacing
there is 0.5, and E86's relative error scales to about 7 nats [X]. Fix: subtract each row's value at a
fixed reference, then sum in groups, and check with `precision_check`. Rounding this size changes
acceptance; it cannot by itself cause a blackjax divergence (threshold 1000), so the notebook must
not claim that it does (audit S7).

**Fix to teach.** Algebra beats hardware: collapse first, then sort, then buy.

**If the failure does not appear.**
- Scatter within 2x of E86's rate: say so plainly. The arithmetic of section 4 still rules out the row-level
  fit at E86's rate, and the VRAM and float32 walls still stand. The lesson moves to those.
- CPU beats the GPU on gather: the price table recommends the CPU. This is a publishable finding,
  and the CPU floors exist so that the claim is fair.
- R1 ties R2 and R3: the charity gets the conjugate table and the notebook's headline is "we declined
  the purchase". The teaching verdict says a big model that changes nothing is the finding.
- R3 on its carrier changes the list: report it, and quote the price of the smallest extension
  that captures it. No extrapolation beyond that carrier.
- Row 3 (`scan`) is not slow on GPU: report it. E86's claim on scan models was never timed.

## 9. Communication lesson

**How to write a price someone can check.** A quote is a sentence with five parts: what is bought,
on what hardware, measured from what, the range, and what would make it wrong. Compare:

> "Two hours on a GPU."

> "One fit of R2 for one forecast month costs [value] min on this workstation, measured from a
> [n]-step pilot; expect [range], and more if [condition]. It moves [k] of 50 flights on the list."

The series rule is stated here: every large notebook opens with its price table and the smallest
model that could answer the question, and says whether the big model changed the decision. A second
lesson is the words around a list: a watch list says "likely to be flagged", never "chronically
delayed", and states that it is an estimate.

| `anchor` | P0, P1 results | Rerun |

| `memory` | Peaks, and the failed cell | Each cell a fresh process |

| `r2_origin_{i}` | Thinned decision draws, nutpie zarr `LocalStore` | Redo that origin; nutpie resume is untested and may not be assumed |

| `r3_subset` | Thinned decision draws | That fit only |
| `decide` | Scores, lists, price table | Rerun from stored forecasts |
| `banner` | All 11 rows, labelled; `write_manifest`, `verify` | Rerun |

blackjax `chees_adaptation` runs warmup as one `scan`, so it cannot be checkpointed inside. Warmup
runs in fixed blocks, positions saved after each, and each block is a **fresh** warmup, not a
