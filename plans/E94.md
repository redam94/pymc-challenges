## 1. The question

Election forecasts combine information that arrives unevenly. A midterm has about a thousand national
generic-ballot polls, a few hundred Senate polls spread over thirty races, and district polls in a third
of the House seats at most, half of them sponsored by a party or a campaign. Most districts have no poll
at all. What every district does have is fundamentals: its partisan lean, whether an incumbent runs,
how much money each side has raised, and the president's party's habit of losing ground in midterms.
The notebook builds one model that lets each source speak where it exists and pools where it does not,
checks it honestly on 2018 and 2022, then freezes a 2026 forecast on election day with a section that
scores it once results exist.

Money runs through the whole story in three roles. As a **forecast signal**, the log ratio of receipts
by the eve predicts vote share beyond lean and incumbency. As a **cause**, it is mostly confounded:
Jacobson's paradox (naive regressions say incumbent spending hurts) arises because money follows close
races. Levitt's (1994) rematches between the same two candidates difference out candidate quality and
district, leaving a small effect with honest bounds. As a **quantity to nowcast**, total spending through
election day is not known on election day: candidates report their October spending a month later.

E94 builds on E40 (2016 presidential poll aggregation: reverse random walk, house effects, shared polling
error, rotation of the unidentified pair) and does not repeat its derivations; it cites E40 for them.
Overlap with E36 (MRP) is avoided: no survey microdata. E63's tone for causal claims (what a design buys,
what it does not) is the model for the money part.

## 3. Models

### 3.1 National environment (part B)

The target is the national House two-party margin N (D minus R, points). Prior: the previous cycle's
margin plus the mean swing against the president's party in earlier midterms (1978-2014 from MEDSL,
2018 and 2022 from the FEC; only cycles before the target): for 2026, 2024's margin -2.6 (Wikipedia's
2024 national table) plus 7.0 (sd 5.9, 12 midterms) gives N ~ Normal(4.3, 5.9). Generic polls (last 22
weeks) measure a weekly reverse random walk anchored at N, plus pollster house effects (ZeroSumNormal),
plus a shared polling error with prior sd from the generic and Senate poll errors of earlier cycles
(3.3 points for 2026), plus excess noise. Poll values are two-party margins with binomial standard errors.

**Failure and fix, as in E40:** N and the shared error are seen only as their sum. Sampled directly,
r_hat reached 1.05 with ESS 130 in the 2026 pilot. Rotating the two standard normals by the angle of
their prior scales gave r_hat 1.007, ESS 480, no divergences. The notebook shows this briefly and
cites E40 for the derivation.

2026 has aggregator snapshots only (DDHQ, FiftyPlusOne, RCP, Silver Bulletin, VoteHub, Race to the WH,
NBC). They enter as weekly measurements with aggregator effects and an assumed sd of 1 point plus excess
noise. They are not independent polls, so the notebook keeps one snapshot per aggregator every other week
in the final version (the pilot used all weekly snapshots).

### 3.2 Races (part C)

Margin of race i in cycle c:
`m_i = N_c + gamma_office + s_int z_c,office + beta_office . (lean/10, incumbency +-1/0, log money ratio) + s_state z_c,state + sigma_office e_i`.

- The coefficients, residual scales and state-effect scale are learned from **earlier cycles only**
  (2016 for 2018; 2016-2020 for 2022; 2016-2022 for 2026), where N_c is the known national margin.
  In the target cycle N is the latent national margin from 3.1, and the state effects are unknown and
  shared by the House districts and the Senate race in a state: this is the correlated race error.
  Census-region effects were in the sketch; the pilot uses states only (one level is enough at 4 cycles).
- Money is `log((D receipts + $50k) / (R receipts + $50k))` from reports received by the eve
  (final cycle totals in 2016 and 2020, which have no report file here: stated). It is a forecast
  signal, and the coefficient is never read as an effect of spending (that is part E).
- Previous margin is dropped: 2022 redistricting and the 2025-26 mid-decade maps (AL, CA, FL, MO, NC,
  OH, TX, UT and others) put most previous margins on other lines. Lean is on the current lines.
- Race polls (last 10 weeks) measure `m_i + (walk_t - N) + shared error + race-level poll error
  (sd from earlier Senate polls, 5.7) + pollster house effect + d_part x sponsor sign`, with binomial
  standard errors and excess noise. Only polled races carry a latent margin in the sampler; every other
  race is simulated from the posterior afterwards (cheap).
- Uncontested races (one major party on the ballot, including California D-vs-D) are certain. Jungle
  ballots (Mississippi's 2018 special, Louisiana, Georgia's 2020 special: `split`) are left out of
  training. Louisiana's 2026 November House ballot is a jungle (`multi_cands`): it is kept at the party
  level with a sentence that its share is not a two-party vote. Alaska uses the final ranked-choice
  round where the FEC reports one.
- Seats and control by simulation: House D seats incl. certain seats, P(D >= 218); Senate D-caucus seats
  incl. those not up (34 in 2026), P(D >= 51) with VP Vance breaking ties (2018: VP Pence; 2022: VP Harris).

### 3.3 Baselines (part D)

- **Fundamentals only**: the same model with no polls at all (national from the midterm prior).
- **Aggregate only**: national information from a weekly 14-day rolling mean of the generic polls (the
  stand-in for 2026's aggregators), race polls as usual.
- **Polls only** (not piloted; NumPy): a race's poll average with its empirical error where polls
  exist, the generic-poll average plus lean elsewhere.

### 3.4 Money and votes (part E)

- **Jacobson's paradox**: 2018 and 2022 House incumbents in contested races (n = 663), incumbent share
  on log10 incumbent and challenger disbursements. Pilot: incumbent coefficient -0.7 points per tenfold
  spending, challenger -7.6; adding lean and a cycle term turns the incumbent coefficient to +1.0 and
  the challenger's to -3.6.
- **Why**: incumbent spending against the final margin (correlation of log spending with |margin| is
  -0.30) and a picture of spending by lean: money goes where the race is close.
- **Rematches** (Levitt 1994): change in D share against change in log D and log R disbursements, with
  a national swing per cycle and slopes varying by cycle around a common mean (hierarchical), Student-t
  errors. 232 rematches in 2006, 2008, 2010, 2014, 2016, 2018, 2020 (excluding redistricting cycles and
  court redraws: GA, TX 2006; FL, NC, VA 2016; PA 2018; NC 2020). Pilot: +0.94 points per e-fold of D
  spending (89% interval 0.41-1.50), -0.21 for R (-0.71 to 0.36). The centred pilot had 33 divergences
  and tau ESS 79: the notebook uses non-centred slopes, or slopes on the spending ratio only. Bounds to
  state: candidate quality can still change between meetings (a scandal, a better campaign), rematches
  are a selected sample (a loser who tries again), and spending is measured as cycle totals including
  primaries.

### 3.5 Spending nowcast (part F)

Target: for each general-election D and R nominee, disbursements over the cycle through the end of the
post-general (30G) coverage, 20 days after the election (Nov 26, 2018; Nov 28, 2022; Nov 23, 2026);
plus general-election IEs dated through election day. "Through election day" itself is never reported:
the 30G covers 12G-end to 20 days after, and the notebook says so.

What is known by the eve: every report received by then (quarterlies and the 12G pre-general, which
covers to Oct 17, 2018 / Oct 19, 2022 / Oct 14, 2026), and IEs received by then (24/48-hour reports).

Findings that shaped the design:

- **Outside money is almost fully visible by the eve.** General-election IEs first received by the eve
  were 99.6% (2018: $1,045M of $1,049M) and 99.2% (2022: $1,610M of $1,623M) of the final dollars. The
  dollar-weighted reporting lag has median 1 day, 90th percentile 2 days. What is missing is dated in
  the last two days and on election day (0.2% and 1.8% of the last week's dollars).
- **Candidates' own late spending is the dark part**: $443M (2018) and $435M (2022) reported in the
  30G, about a fifth of the cycle's nominee spending, arriving a month after the election.

Models (both piloted):

- **Late candidate spending**: `log1p(30G disbursements) ~ Normal(a_g + b1_g (log 20-day spending at the
  12G rate) + b2 log cash on hand + b_inc incumbent, sd_g)`, groups g = office x competitiveness
  (|lean| < 8, 8-20, > 20), partially pooled. Student-t errors were tried first and failed: per-candidate
  coverage was fine but the tails summed to absurd totals (2022 predicted at $2.1B, 90% up to
  $4.7 x 10^11). Normal errors on the log scale fixed it.
- **Cycle-level shift**: fitted on 2018 the model predicted 2022's late total at $449M (90% $374-548M;
  true $434M); fitted on 2022 it predicted 2018 at $606M ($480-790M; true $443M). A shared shift per
  cycle explains the miss, and two cycles cannot estimate its size, so the nowcast adds a cycle effect
  with an assumed sd of 0.25 on the log scale and says so.
- **IE lag**: dollars disseminated L days before the eve (L = 0-7, in $100k units), fraction received by
  the eve, beta-binomial with `logit f_L = a + b log1p(L)` (pilot a = 0.22, b = 2.0), plus the
  election-day share of the last week's dollars. A cycle-level overdispersion term on that share
  replaces the pilot's binomial (0.2% vs 1.8% in the two cycles).
- **Backtest**: truncate filings at each eve by receipt date, nowcast, compare with the totals as filed
  by 2026-11-03: national total interval, per-candidate PIT and 90% coverage (pilot 0.93-0.94: slightly
  too wide per candidate, while the total was off in one direction), and per-race totals.
- The sketch's "hierarchical growth curve over the cycle" was replaced: by the eve the curve is observed
  up to mid-October for every candidate, so only its tail is unknown, and the burn rate in the 12G period
  plus cash on hand predict that tail. The full curve (cumulative spending by week, eve-known against
  final) is still the opening figure of part F.

Proxy and what it loses: the report file keeps only the current version of each report. A report amended
after the eve has the amendment's receipt date and numbers, so it drops out of "known by the eve"
(11 of 1,018 12G reports in 2018, 21 of 1,136 in 2022) and reports amended before the eve carry amended
numbers. The IE file keeps amendment chains, and as-of-eve IE is rebuilt from filings received by then;
the duplicate merge (same spender, candidate, amount and date) is a heuristic.

## 7. The 2026 freeze and later scoring (parts G and H)

- The 2026 model spec is locked as piloted (tools/e94_pilot/elec1.py 2026 full, spend3.py, ie1.py) and
  the frozen file is the forecast that gets scored. The notebook recomputes the forecast with the same

- The scoring section loads results when they exist and prints "not yet available" otherwise; the
  code path is exercised on 2018/2022 with the same functions. What will be compared: House and Senate

## 9. What the data cannot support

- Individual 2026 national polls, so no 2026 house effects or sample sizes for the national part;
  aggregators are averages of overlapping polls, not independent measurements.
- Presidential vote by district: lean (538) and PVI (Cook) are blends, not raw votes.
- Previous margins in redistricted states, and a clean district history across 2022 and 2026 lines.
- Two backtest cycles: the shared polling error and the spending cycle shift are each seen twice; the
  calibration of the national part rests mainly on priors from earlier cycles.
- A causal effect of money outside the rematch design; the forecast coefficient on money is a signal.
- Spending "through election day" exactly: the 30G period runs 20 days past it.
- Report versions as of the eve for candidate reports (current versions only).
- 48-hour contribution notices; party coordinated spending and non-IE outside money.
- Internal-poll flags for 2026.

## 10. Design changes from the sketch

1. Previous margin dropped; lean only (with the 2 x PVI scale verified).
2. Region effects replaced by state effects shared by House and Senate.
3. Fundamentals coefficients learned from earlier cycles only, so every backtest is out of sample.
4. Polling error priors from 538's raw polls of earlier cycles.
5. The N-vs-shared-error rotation from E40 (needed: r_hat 1.05 -> 1.007).
6. 2026's aggregator-only national information mirrored by an aggregate-only backtest.
7. Spending nowcast: IE needs only a small lag correction; the model effort goes to candidates' late
   spending, with Normal (not Student-t) log errors and an explicit cycle shift.
8. The frozen pilot is the scored 2026 forecast.
